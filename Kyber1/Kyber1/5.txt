Vehicular ad hoc networks enable vehicles to communicate with each other and with roadside infrastructure in real time.
Such communications support numerous applications, including collision avoidance, traffic optimization, and autonomous driving.

However, the open and dynamic nature of vehicular networks makes them vulnerable to various security threats,
such as eavesdropping, message tampering, and replay attacks.
These threats become even more severe in a post-quantum context, where traditional cryptographic assumptions may no longer hold.

To address these challenges, this work adopts a hybrid encryption approach that integrates a post-quantum key encapsulation
mechanism with symmetric encryption to protect vehicular data transmissions.
